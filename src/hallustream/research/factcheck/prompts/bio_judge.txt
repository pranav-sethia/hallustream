You are a careful fact-checker. A language model was asked to write a short biography of {name}. Check its biography against the Wikipedia passages about {name} given below.

Do two things.

1. ENTITIES. Go through the biography in order and extract every specific entity:
- people (names, affiliations)
- organizations
- locations
- dates and events
- specific quantities or statistics
- titles of works, awards, citations and references

For each entity, give the minimal text span, copied exactly from the biography: just the entity itself (e.g. "1867", not "born in 1867"). Keep its accents, apostrophes, quote marks, dashes and capitalization, and never include markdown symbols (*, _, #, backticks) or list bullets. List entities in the order they appear; if the same entity appears several times, list each appearance separately. Do not list one entity inside another (for "University of Paris", list only "University of Paris", not also "Paris").

Write a short note (at most 15 words) on what the passages say, then label the entity as it is used in its sentence:
- "Supported": the passages confirm it.
- "Not Supported": the passages contradict it; or the passages cover this aspect of the person (such as birth date or place, education, positions, main works, awards) and give something different or leave it out where Wikipedia would plainly list it; or it is a person, work or organization you are confident does not exist.
- "Insufficient": the passages are silent on it, and it is a minor detail that Wikipedia would not necessarily mention.
You may use your own knowledge to mark an entity "Not Supported", but never to mark it "Supported".

2. CLAIMS. Go through the biography sentence by sentence and break each sentence into all the independent atomic facts it contains (usually 2 to 5 per sentence). Each claim is a short, self-contained sentence stating exactly one fact about {name}, with pronouns replaced by the name. Split as finely as possible: "He made his acting debut in the film Sunrise (1992)" gives "He made his acting debut in a film.", "His debut film was Sunrise.", "Sunrise is a film.", "Sunrise was released in 1992." Include every statement, including vague or general ones. Write a short note (at most 15 words), then label each claim:
- "Supported": the passages support it.
- "Not Supported": the passages contradict it or do not mention it, or it is not about {name} (for example, it describes a different person with the same name).

Rules:
- Ignore small technicalities; we want facts the model got wrong or made up.
- If the biography only says it does not know the person or cannot find information, return empty lists. If it hedges ("may have", "possibly") but still names specifics, judge those specifics as usual.
- The biography may stop in the middle of a sentence. Do not extract an entity that is cut off, and do not write claims from the unfinished final sentence.
